Early Treatment of Cancer: A Randomized Controlled Trial

Journal of Health Economics, 2023

Background: Many patients with cancer remain asymptomatic for years before clinical presentation. We evaluated whether early treatment in screen-detected asymptomatic patients improves outcomes compared to waiting for symptomatic presentation.

Methods: 2,400 patients with screen-detected asymptomatic disease were randomized to immediate treatment vs. watchful waiting. Primary outcome was progression to severe disease at 5 years.

Results: Immediate treatment reduced progression to severe disease (12% vs. 28%, p<0.001). Treatment was well-tolerated with similar adverse event rates in both groups. In the watchful waiting arm, 65% of patients eventually developed symptoms and were then offered treatment. Among symptomatic patients who received treatment, progression to severe disease occurred in 35% vs. 12% in those treated while asymptomatic (p<0.001).

Conclusion: Early treatment of asymptomatic disease X substantially improves long-term outcomes compared to waiting for symptomatic presentation.

Performance of the Rapid Cancer Screening Test in Community Settings Diagnostic Medicine, 2024

Background: Within our community, 8% of people will get Cancer. Currently, individuals that get Cancer can only be treated with the one time necessary surgery when they are symptomatic. A new screening test has been created that can test for Cancer. We evaluated the QuickScreen-Cancer test ($45) in 5,000 community-dwelling adults across 12 primary care clinics.

Methods:  Within each of the 12 primary care facilities, all 5,000 participants completed a health background survey to analyze their risk level. Participants were then given a QuickScreen-Cancer test to determine if they had Cancer.  The results came back within 2 hours of providing the test and were able to be completed in a basic phlebotomy lab. Those with positive results were provided the one time surgery for treatment. Growths that were removed from individuals during the surgery then had further testing to determine if they were positive for cancer. Individuals that had negative results were monitored over 5 years to determine if Cancer developed.

Results:  Of the 5,000 participants, we found that around X% of individuals tested positive for Cancer. Of the growths removed, 92% (95% CI: 88-95%) were found to have Cancer. From the individuals that tested negative, 0 of the participants got Cancer in the next five years. The test performed equally well across all subgroups examined.

American Society for Cancer Management: Screening Recommendations (2024)

RISK STRATIFICATION:

High-risk patients are defined as individuals with ≥2 of the following:

  • High Blood Pressure (> 140/90 )
  • Family history of Cancer
  • Existence of a growth
  • History of Cancer causing workplace

Approximately 35% of the general adult population meets high-risk criteria.

SCREENING PROTOCOLS:

  • Recommended at age 45
  • One time screening

TREATMENT INITIATION:

Current evidence supports immediate treatment (growth removal surgery) initiation upon diagnosis. Delays in treatment are associated with worse outcomes. Individuals over 50 cannot be treated due to the risks of the surgery.

INTERVIEW WITH DR. SARAH CHEN Community Health Director

Q: Can you walk me through how your clinic identifies and treats patients with Cancer?

A: We currently treat people that come in complaining of symptoms - you know, the classic fatigue, joint pain, brain fog. But we are catching people pretty late in the disease course.

About two years ago, we implemented the risk survey. This allowed us to identify high-risk patients - anyone over 45 with a family history or other risk factors. We flag them in our EMR system.That’s been working pretty well. However, there is no confirmatory test so there are patients that are getting the costly surgery treatment that may not need it. Unfortunately, if a patient is over 50 years old, the treatment surgery is too dangerous to perform in a community health center. This makes catching cases early really important.

Q: What happens with patients that are not treated before 50?

A: We can provide those patients palliative care with things like pain and nausea medication. A small subset of those patients may still get the surgery at a larger health facility. The patients that cannot get treatment will eventual progress to a more severe stage of Cancer where they will have more hospital visits and often get blood transfusions to help with the anemia. Patients with a more severe stage of cancer they will also have a risk of death from the Cancer.

Q: What about the costs involved?

A: Treatment costs between $800 - $1,200. Insurance usually covers everything. Patients that are caught later tend to have more expensive treatment because it takes the clinicians longer to remove all the growth. I’ve heard the government is looking into covering a new screening test that is only $45 per test.

Excerpt from: “Real-world implementation of universal screening for Cancer”

Our community clinic implemented universal screening at routine preventive care visits. All adults aged 45 presenting for annual wellness visits were offered screening.

Implementation details:

  • Screening incorporated into standard bloodwork panels
  • Medical assistants flagged eligible patients during check-in
  • Results typically available within 2 hours
  • Positive results triggered automatic referral to treatment protocol
  • Average time from positive test to treatment initiation: 8 days
